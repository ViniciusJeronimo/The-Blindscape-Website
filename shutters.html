Plantation Shutters

Plantation shutters bring a more architectural, built-in feel than most other window furnishings. They look deliberate, clean and high-end.

Timeless style Strong light control Premium finish

Best for: living rooms, bedrooms, street-facing windows and homes where the visual finish matters just as much as function.

Basswood Shutters

Basswood shutters are about elegance and a refined internal look. They are often the better fit when the customer prioritises style and premium presentation.

Lightweight feel Elegant finish Premium interior look

Best for: dry indoor areas, premium homes and clients who want the shutters to feel more refined than purely practical.

PVC Shutters

PVC shutters lean harder into durability and practicality, especially in areas where moisture resistance matters more. They still need to look good, but function leads.

Moisture resistant Practical choice Durable finish

Best for: bathrooms, laundries and spaces where exposure makes practicality more important.

Custom Fit & Finishing

With shutters, finish quality matters. Frame choice, blade size and overall fit are the difference between a product that feels premium and one that feels wrong for the window.

Tailored fit Architectural look Higher-end outcome

Best for: customers who care about detail, proportion and getting a polished result that suits the room properly.

FAQs

Questions That Help Customers Commit

Common questions about plantation shutters in Perth.

Are shutters worth the higher price?

For many customers, yes. They offer a more built-in premium feel, strong control of light and privacy, and a more timeless finished look than many other options.

What is the difference between basswood and PVC shutters?

Basswood generally leans more refined and design-led, while PVC is often the better practical choice where moisture resistance is more important.

Do shutters suit modern homes?

Yes, when the frame style, blade size and overall fit are chosen properly. They can feel both timeless and very clean-lined in modern interiors.

Can shutters work in wet areas?

Yes, that is where PVC-style solutions usually make more sense than timber-style options.
